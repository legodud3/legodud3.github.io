# What is a Calorie?

We have become much more conscious of food labels and read the back of pack, focussing especially on one number: calories per serving. The calorie is actually a unit of energy. The energy in food is actually measured and reported in kilocalories ( what we mistakenly call "calories"). The average male requires 2000-2500 kcal per day. But what does this number even mean?

First off, 1 KILOcalorie = 1000 calories (duh for non-US readers).

* 100 KILOcalories i.e. a very small snack is enough to charge your phone from 0 to 100% EIGHT times.Phones are so energy efficient.  
* 2000 KILOcalories i.e. a day of eating is enough to bring 20 liters of ice cold water to a boil (but not boil it because latent heat of vaporization is a thing.) 
* 15,000 KILOcalories i.e. what you eat in a week is enough to put a 1kg satellite into low-earth orbit. With some energy leftover. 

The human body, on average, has about 100,000 kcal of stored energy (mostly in your liver)... 50 days of "reserve power". It is pretty good at converting chemical-to-mechanical energy.
