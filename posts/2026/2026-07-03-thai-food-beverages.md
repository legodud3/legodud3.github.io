First, an admission, I have not written a lot over the last 1 month. I can't make many excuses but for what it's worth we took Samay on his first international trip to Thailand in mid June (excellent!). I have had an aversion to Thai food because I had never had Thai food in Thailand. Also, I am allergic to shellfish so that rules out approximately 3/4th of their food (fish & oyster sauce is like salt - its in everything).
In any case, our food choices on this trip were exceptional 10/10. The most interesting observation I had about restaurants and street vendors in Thailand is that many of them are organized by protein type -- shops that sell only chicken, only pork, only fish, only beef, and so on. Many restaurants also have...quite literal names, e.g. a famous chain in Bangkok is called "Kub Kao Kub Pla" which is a popular Thai phrase that literally means "dishes to eat with rice".

Another interesting discovery was that of the Michelin "Bib Gourmand" list (https://guide.michelin.com/en/restaurants/bib-gourmand). While I enjoy food, I definitely do not have the palate to appreciate a 1/2/3 Michelin star meal. However, for people like me, Michelin has come up with this handy Bib Gourmand tag which represents "great food at an affordable price". Restaurants and street vendors in Thailand will proudly display that they were given the Michellin Bib Gourmand tag. Now don't get confused by a random "Michellin Guide" sign - that just means they were in the Guide i.e. "they are good" vs the Bib Gourmand bag i.e. "they are banger".

Anyway, some of my favorites were:

- Beef noodles from Ket-o-cha (Bib Gourmand). These guys know broth, trust them blindly.
- Thai teas in all forms (incl taro etc.). It's all the wonder of condensed milk + ice + hot & humid climate. You can keep guzzling these things.
- Mango sticky from anywhere. Because its combining mango (Thailand has excellent fruit) with sticky rice (looks like every Thai makes it everyday)
- Chicken & rice from Go-Ang Pratunam (Bib Gourmand). I could not believe how tasty a two ingredient dish could be, but you have to try it to believe it.
- Pork dumplings from a place whose name I don't remember but is a HK based vendor with a Bib Gourmand. Which brings me to the simplest tip - go to "Gourmet Eats" at Siam Paragon. It is a collection of Bib Gourmand and Michelin guide street vendors & restaurants under one roof. An air conditioned roof, with nice seating, and manageable lines.  And even after paying that premium you will feel as if you paid about 5 times less than you should have.
