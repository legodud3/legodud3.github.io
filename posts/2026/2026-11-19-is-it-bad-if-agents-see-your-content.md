title: "Is it bad if agents see your content?"
date: 2026-11-19
---

## Context 
As a reminder to future me, the primary audience of this blog is me. And why I write was written down by past me over at [what writing improves](/p/2025-05-01-what-writing-improves.html). A few months ago, I (technically, chatGPT) wired up Google Analytics on this blog. Every morning, I get a message of the prior day: (views, visitors, avg time spent). As you can imagine, on most days that message is (0,0,0). Then, one day that message had some non-zero numbers. I was quite happy with myself! 2 views from France !? You don't say. Well well well, maybe my writing is useful to people who are not me. 

## Non-zero days
Then, I had more than a few "non-zero" days. Yesterday (Nov 18th, 2026) that vector was (20, 20, 8 seconds). Which is the most it has ever been. Hmm. This is unexpected. And so I asked myself: Are these agents? Turns out that it's complicated and all I could conclude is that there is no way to tell with certainty if these are humans or agents.

Then I asked myself: Does it matter? And my answer is no, it does not matter. 

Everyone sees attention from humans as 'GOOD' and ascribes a positive value to it. You created or put out something other humans consider valuable. You build your life, identity, and in many cases your business on this notion. 
We are now living in a world where agents browse, consume, and interact with the internet alongside humans. Yet, most people see attention from agents as 'BAD', ascribe a *negative* value to this attention, and actively try to block agents. My opinion is simple: ALL attention is good. Even the ones from agents. Why? Because getting an agents' attention also implies that you have created something valuable. It's just that the positive value associated with it may be a different number. But never forget, it's still a +ve number. Some may even argue that it's a bigger +ve number to get an agents' attention than a humans' but lets leave that aside for now.

Suppose one of those two views from France was an agent looking up something for someone. And suppose what I wrote helped it give them a better answer. I think I’d still be quite happy with myself. 

## The other side
What if I'm wrong? What if an agent's attention is actually a -ve value event? We will end up creating an agent-friendly internet anyway. Consider where things are headed:

- There will be more agents; not fewer
- They will browse, consume, and interact more; not less
- People will change what they put out and how they monetize it to absorb this -ve value event. Because it cannot be avoided. Kinda like taxes, or spoilage, or someone stealing your stuff. You don't have to like it. You just have to price it in.
