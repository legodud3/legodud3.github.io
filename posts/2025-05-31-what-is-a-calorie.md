# What is a Calorie?

We have become much more conscious of food labels and read the back of pack, focussing especially on one number: calories per serving. The calorie is actually a unit of energy. The energy in food is actually measured and reported in kilocalories ( what we mistakenly call "calories"). The average male requires 2000-2500 kcal per day. But what does this number even mean?

First off, 1 KILOcalorie = 1000 calories (duh).

* 100 KILOcalories i.e. a very small snack is enough to keep your phone charged—FULLY CHARGED—for 1 day! Phones are so energy efficient. And food is so energy dense. 
* 2000 KILOcalories i.e. a day of eating is enough to boil 20 liters of ice-cold water. 
* 15,000 KILOcalories i.e. what you eat in a week is enough to put a 1kg satellite into low-earth orbit via a typical Electric Vehicle. 

The human body, on average, has about 100,000 kcal of stored energy... 50 days of "reserve power."
